Photosensitivity Warning

This application produces rapidly flashing lights designed to simulate the Brion Gysin Dream Machine — a stroboscopic device for visual brainwave entrainment.

Do not use if you:

  • Have epilepsy or a history of seizures
  • Have a family history of epilepsy
  • Are prone to photosensitive reactions

For best results: dark room, eyes closed, face toward screen. Headphones or speakers for audio.

How It Works

Flickering light drives your visual cortex — an effect called photic driving: the cortex tends to lock onto the flicker rate, and many people see geometric patterns, colour, and drifting imagery behind closed eyelids. A matched pulsing tone adds an auditory channel. Responses vary a lot from person to person.

For best results: dark room, eyes closed, face toward the screen.

Frequency & Bands

The flicker rate in cycles per second (Hz). Manual is a free sandbox from 1 Hz up to half your display's refresh rate — the fastest a screen can flicker without aliasing (≈30 Hz on a 60 Hz display, ≈72 Hz on a 144 Hz one). The readout names the EEG band:

  • Delta (1–4 Hz) — very slow; deep-sleep range.
  • Theta (4–8 Hz) — drowsy, meditative, hypnagogic.
  • Alpha (8–13 Hz) — relaxed but awake. ~10 Hz is the classic target and the most reliable for geometric imagery.
  • Beta (13–30 Hz) — alert, finer detail. Note: ~15–25 Hz is the peak seizure-risk band (see Safety).
  • Gamma (30 Hz+) — fast; the 40 Hz preset lives here.

Waveform (visual)

  • Sine — smooth fade in and out. Gentlest on the eyes; the default.
  • Triangle — linear ramp up/down. Closest to the original slotted Dreamachine.
  • Square — hard on/off flashes. Sharpest transients and strongest drive, but the harshest.

Audio

A 220 Hz tone driven at the flicker rate. Pick the method:

  • Isochronic — a single tone pulsed on/off. Strongest entrainment and the default; works on any speakers.
  • Monaural — two close tones beating together. Works on speakers.
  • Binaural 🎧 — a different tone in each ear; the beat forms in your head. Requires headphones — it does nothing on speakers.
  • Off — visual only (plus any music you load).

Link ties the audio rate to the visual flicker (the natural pairing). Switch it off to give the audio its own frequency slider — handy for playing one rate against another.

Modes

  • Manual — the sandbox: set the frequency freely with the slider. Use Light to switch the strobe off for an audio-only session, and Link to decouple the audio from the visual.
  • Descent — a guided journey stepping 12→4 Hz over the session: from alert alpha, through the 10 Hz sweet spot, down into theta. Default 20 minutes. Load an audio file to sync the sweep to your track's length.
  • Gamma 40 Hz — a fixed 40 Hz tone, the rate used in gamma-stimulation research. 40 Hz light needs a fast (120 Hz+) display; on a 60 Hz screen the light can't show 40 Hz cleanly, so it stays steady while the audio runs at 40 Hz.

Audio file

Load a local track (♫) to play under the session — nothing leaves your browser. In Descent it sets the session length, and the first 60 seconds fade the strobe and tone in so your music leads.

Safety

Flickering light can trigger seizures in people with photosensitive epilepsy — risk is highest in roughly the 15–25 Hz range. Manual lets you dial into and above that band, so treat it with care. The light uses amber/white rather than the more provocative saturated red, and the slider can't exceed half your refresh rate (so there's no hidden aliasing to a lower, riskier rate); the 40 Hz Gamma rate sits above the peak-risk band. None of this removes the risk. Do not use this if you have epilepsy, a seizure history, or are photosensitive. If you feel any discomfort, stop immediately.
